# Competitor Intel

Produce competitive intelligence a founder can act on: not a feature spreadsheet for its own sake, but answers to "how do we win against these players, and what should we copy, counter, or ignore?"

## When to use

- "Analyze my competitors", "how does X position vs Y", "build a battlecard", "how do I differentiate"
- Market entry research, pricing benchmarking, sales enablement

## Process

### 1. Scope

Establish:
- The user's product, ICP, and current positioning
- The competitor list (or discover it: direct competitors, indirect substitutes, and "do nothing/spreadsheet" — the most common competitor)
- The decision this research feeds: positioning? pricing? sales objections? roadmap?

Cap active analysis at 3-5 competitors; more becomes noise. Group the rest.

### 2. Research (use web access when available)

Per competitor, gather from public sources:
- **Positioning**: homepage headline, who they target, category claim, key differentiators they push
- **Pricing**: model, tiers, price points, free tier/trial mechanics, what's gated where
- **Product**: flagship capabilities, notable gaps, integrations, platform
- **Go-to-market**: channels visibly working (SEO footprint, ads, content, community, sales-led vs self-serve), recent launches/funding
- **Voice of customer**: review sites (G2/Capterra/Trustpilot), Reddit/HN threads, app store reviews — mine the *complaints* and the "switched from/to" stories; these are gold
- Cite sources; mark inferences as inferences.

### 3. Analyze

- **Positioning map**: 2 axes that matter to buyers (e.g. price vs. depth, SMB vs. enterprise) — place everyone including the user
- **Per competitor**: strengths (be honest), weaknesses (from customer complaints, not wishful thinking), and their likely next move
- **The gaps**: segments underserved, complaints nobody's fixing, positioning territory unclaimed

### 4. Deliver

Default deliverable:

```markdown
# Competitive Analysis — [Market]

## TL;DR
3-5 sentences: the state of the field and the single biggest opportunity.

## Landscape table
| Competitor | Target | Positioning | Pricing | Key strength | Exploitable weakness |

## Deep dives (per main competitor)
Positioning, pricing, GTM, customer complaints (quoted), threat level.

## Where we win / where we lose
Honest, evidence-based. Losing scenarios matter more than winning ones.

## Recommendations
Positioning angle, pricing implications, roadmap signals, messaging to use (and avoid).
```

**Battlecard mode** (when for sales): one page per competitor — "why we win" (3 bullets with proof), "why we lose" (honest), landmine questions to plant, objection responses, pricing comparison, and "don't say" list.

## Rules

- Ethical sources only: public information, reviews, published pricing. No pretexting, no scraping behind logins, no misrepresentation.
- Customer complaints about competitors are the highest-value data — always mine reviews.
- Resist feature-parity conclusions; recommend differentiation, not imitation, unless a gap is table-stakes.
- Date the research; competitive facts go stale. Note anything likely to change.
