# Strategic Planning

Help a founder decide what to do, and — more importantly — what NOT to do. The output is a plan with one clear priority, measurable targets, and explicit trade-offs, not a wish list.

## When to use

- "Help me plan Q3", "set OKRs", "what should I focus on", "I have 5 ideas and can't pick"
- Annual planning, post-mortem + reset, pivot decisions

## Process

### 1. Establish the current position

Gather (ask in one batch, only what's missing):
- **Stage & numbers**: revenue/users, growth rate, runway, team size
- **What's working**: the channel/product/segment with real traction
- **What's dragging**: where time goes without results
- **The horizon**: planning a quarter? a year? a decision?
- **The founder's own goal**: sell in 3 years? lifestyle business? venture scale? This changes every answer.

### 2. Diagnose before planning

Apply the strategy kernel (diagnosis → guiding policy → coherent actions):
- **What is the single bottleneck?** At any moment a business has one dominant constraint: awareness, conversion, retention, capacity, or cash. Name it with evidence.
- Stress-test the founder's assumptions: if they say "we need more leads" but churn is 10%/mo, the diagnosis is retention.

### 3. Choose the ONE priority

- Propose the single most important objective for the horizon, derived from the bottleneck.
- List the top 2-3 candidates that were NOT chosen and say explicitly why — the "not-doing list" is a deliverable.
- Kill or park zombie projects: anything that's neither working nor strategic.

### 4. Build the plan

Structure:

```markdown
# [Quarter/Year] Plan — [Company]

## Diagnosis
The bottleneck and the evidence for it. 3-5 sentences.

## Objective
One sentence. Qualitative and inspiring but concrete.

## Key results (2-4)
Measurable, with baseline → target ("Trial→paid: 8% → 12%").

## Initiatives (3-5 max)
For each: what, owner, why it moves a key result, definition of done.

## Explicitly NOT doing
The parked list, with one-line reasons.

## Weekly scoreboard
The 3-5 numbers to review every week.

## Risks & tripwires
"If X hasn't happened by [date], we [decision]."
```

### 5. Pressure-test

Before delivering, check:
- Can the team actually execute this with current capacity? Cut until yes.
- Does every initiative trace to the objective? Remove orphans.
- Are key results outcomes (revenue, conversion, retention) rather than output ("ship feature X")? Outputs go in initiatives.

## Rules

- One objective per horizon. If the user insists on three, force-rank them.
- Be direct about hard calls — a strategy that avoids the uncomfortable decision isn't one.
- Plans must survive contact with a calendar: 3-5 initiatives max for a small team.
- Always include tripwires: pre-committed decision points beat endless persistence.
