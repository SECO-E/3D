# Brand Copywriter

Write copy that sounds like one particular company and sells one particular thing. The enemy is interchangeable copy — words that could sit on any competitor's site without anyone noticing.

## When to use

- Website copy (homepage, about, product/feature pages), taglines, product descriptions
- "Define our brand voice", "our copy sounds generic", "rewrite this page"

## Process

### 1. Extract the raw material

Copy quality is limited by input quality. Gather:
- What the product does and the #1 outcome customers get
- Who buys it, and the words THEY use (ask for support tickets, reviews, sales-call phrases — voice-of-customer language beats invented copy)
- What makes it genuinely different (push past "quality and service" — a differentiator competitors could also claim isn't one)
- Personality targets: 3 adjectives they want + 2 they explicitly don't ("confident but not arrogant")
- Existing copy, if rewriting

### 2. Define the voice (when needed)

Deliver a compact voice guide:
- **3-4 voice traits**, each with a "this means / this doesn't mean" pair and a sample line
- **Vocabulary**: words we use, words we ban (every brand needs a ban list: "solutions", "seamless", "empower"...)
- **Mechanics**: sentence length tendencies, contractions, humor allowance, how we address the reader
- **Same-message demo**: one message written in the brand voice vs. in generic corporate voice, side by side

### 3. Write the copy

Structure by asset type:

**Homepage**: headline (the #1 outcome or sharpest differentiator, in customer language) → subhead (how, for whom) → social proof strip → 3 benefit blocks (each: benefit headline, 2-3 lines, feature as proof) → objection/how-it-works section → final CTA. One primary CTA repeated.

**Tagline**: generate 10-15 across approaches (outcome, enemy, category redefinition, customer identity, wordplay — clearly marked as risky), then shortlist 3 with rationale.

**Product descriptions**: lead with the job it does, not what it is; specs after benefits; sensory/concrete language over adjectives.

**About page**: it's about the customer's stakes, not the company's history — origin story framed as "why this matters for you", beliefs, team credibility.

### 4. Editing standards (apply to every draft)

- **Specificity test**: replace every claim with a number, example, or concrete detail where possible. "Fast support" → "median reply: 11 minutes".
- **Competitor-swap test**: if a competitor's name fits in the sentence unchanged, cut or sharpen it.
- **One reader**: write to a single person ("you"), never "our customers".
- **Rhythm**: vary sentence length; read aloud; cut adverbs and hedge words.
- **Clarity beats cleverness** everywhere except deliberately playful brands — and even then, clarity in the CTA.

### 5. Deliver

Provide the copy in final, paste-ready form; for headlines and taglines always provide ranked variants; note where real numbers/testimonials must replace placeholders — never leave invented proof in deliverables.

## Rules

- Never invent testimonials, statistics, or claims. Placeholder + note instead.
- Voice-of-customer language is the strongest copy source — actively request it.
- Persuade with concreteness and honesty, not superlatives and pressure.
- Consistency: within a project, every asset follows the same voice guide.
